# Frequently Asked Questions — DGMS and Mine Safety

Q: What does DGMS stand for?
A: Directorate General of Mines Safety. It is the regulator for safety and health
in mines in India, under the Ministry of Labour and Employment, headquartered at
Dhanbad.

Q: Which law governs safety in Indian mines?
A: The Mines Act, 1952, together with rules and regulations made under it (such as
the Mines Rules, 1955; the Coal Mines Regulations, 2017; and the Metalliferous
Mines Regulations, 1961).

Q: What are the maximum working hours in a mine?
A: Above ground, an adult may not work more than 48 hours a week or 9 hours a day
(Section 30). Below ground, the limit is 48 hours a week and 8 hours a day
(Section 31). Work beyond these limits attracts overtime at twice the ordinary
wage (Section 33).

Q: Can women work in mines?
A: Women are prohibited from working below ground (Section 46). Above ground they
may generally be employed only between 6 a.m. and 7 p.m., subject to any
relaxation permitted by the government.

Q: What is the minimum age to work in a mine?
A: No person below 18 years of age may be allowed to work in a mine (Section 40),
and persons below 18 may not be present in parts where mining operations are
carried on (Section 45).

Q: Within what time must an accident be reported?
A: An accident causing loss of life or serious bodily injury must be notified to
the authorities as required under Section 23 of the Mines Act, 1952 (and
Regulation 8 of the Coal Mines Regulations, 2017). For a fatal accident, the
authority must hold an inquiry within two months (Section 23(2)).

Q: What is a Safety Management Plan?
A: A risk-assessment-based plan required under the Coal Mines Regulations, 2017,
that identifies the mine's principal hazards and sets out the controls,
responsibilities and systems to manage them.

Q: What is the difference between coal and metalliferous mine regulations?
A: Coal mines are governed by the Coal Mines Regulations, 2017; non-coal
(metalliferous) mines such as iron ore, limestone, bauxite and metal mines are
governed by the Metalliferous Mines Regulations, 1961.

Q: What is the leading cause of fatal accidents in underground mines?
A: Fall of roof and sides (ground movement) is historically a leading cause,
which is why systematic support rules and roof support are strongly enforced.

Q: What certificates does DGMS grant?
A: DGMS conducts examinations and grants competency certificates such as
Manager (First/Second Class), Overman, Sirdar/Mining Mate, Surveyor, Shotfirer/
Blaster, Winding Engine Driver and Gas Testing.

Q: What is firedamp?
A: Firedamp is inflammable gas, chiefly methane (CH4), found in coal mines. It
can form an explosive mixture with air, so coal mines are classified by degree of
gassiness and methane is continuously monitored.

Q: Is training compulsory before working in a mine?
A: Yes. Under the Mines Vocational Training Rules, 1966, a new worker must receive
basic vocational training before deployment, plus refresher and special training
as required.

Q: What are the Mines Rescue Rules about?
A: The Mines Rescue Rules, 1985 require rescue stations/rooms, trained rescue
brigades, breathing apparatus and periodical training so that trained teams can
respond to fire, explosion or irrespirable-atmosphere emergencies.
