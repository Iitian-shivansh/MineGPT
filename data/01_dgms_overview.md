# DGMS — Directorate General of Mines Safety (Overview)

## What is DGMS
The Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) is the Indian government
regulatory agency responsible for safety and health of workers in mines. It
functions under the Ministry of Labour and Employment, Government of India. Its
headquarters is at Dhanbad, Jharkhand. DGMS is headed by the Director General of
Mines Safety.

DGMS administers and enforces the Mines Act, 1952 and the rules and regulations
framed under it. It covers coal mines, metalliferous (non-coal) mines, and oil
mines across India.

## Main functions of DGMS
- Inspection of mines to enforce safety and health standards.
- Investigation and inquiry into accidents and dangerous occurrences.
- Grant of statutory permissions, exemptions, relaxations and approvals.
- Approval and testing of mining equipment, machinery, explosives-related
  apparatus and safety appliances for use in mines.
- Conduct of examinations and grant of statutory competency certificates
  (for example Manager, Overman, Sirdar/Mining Mate, Surveyor, Gas Testing,
  Winding Engine Driver).
- Promotion of safety awareness, safety education and training.
- Analysis of accident statistics and framing of safety recommendations.
- Advising government on mine safety legislation and policy.

## Key legislation administered by DGMS
- The Mines Act, 1952 (the parent statute).
- The Mines Rules, 1955.
- The Coal Mines Regulations, 2017 (replaced the 1957 regulations).
- The Metalliferous Mines Regulations, 1961.
- The Oil Mines Regulations, 2017 (replaced the 1984 regulations).
- The Mines Rescue Rules, 1985.
- The Mines Vocational Training Rules, 1966.
- The Coal Mines Pit Head Bath Rules, 1959.
- The Mines Creche Rules, 1966.

## Note on the Labour Codes
The Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (OSH Code) has
been enacted to consolidate and replace several labour laws, including the Mines
Act, 1952. Until the OSH Code and its rules are fully brought into force for
mines, the Mines Act, 1952 and the regulations above continue to apply.
