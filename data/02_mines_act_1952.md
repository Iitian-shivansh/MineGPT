# The Mines Act, 1952 — Key Provisions

The Mines Act, 1952 is the principal law regulating labour, safety and health in
Indian mines. It is administered by DGMS under the Ministry of Labour and
Employment. The rules and regulations for mines are made under this Act.

## Scope and definitions (Section 2)
- "Mine" means any excavation where any operation for the purpose of searching
  for or obtaining minerals has been or is being carried on, and includes all
  associated works, machinery, shafts, inclines, railways, workshops, etc.
- "Adult" means a person who has completed 18 years of age.
- "Agent", "Manager", "Owner" are defined with their statutory responsibilities.
- The Act applies to coal mines, metalliferous mines and oil mines.

## Authorities
- Chief Inspector of Mines and Inspectors (DGMS officers) are appointed under
  Section 5 to administer the Act. The Director General of Mines Safety is the
  Chief Inspector of Mines for the country.
- Committees and Courts of Inquiry can be constituted to investigate accidents.

## Notice of accidents (Section 23)
- Section 23(1): When an accident causing loss of life or serious bodily injury,
  or a specified dangerous occurrence, takes place in or about a mine, the
  manager must give notice to the authorities in the prescribed form and post a
  copy on the special notice board.
- Section 23(2): Where the notice relates to an accident causing loss of life,
  the authority must make an inquiry into the accident within two months.
- Failure to give the required notice or to post it is a punishable offence.

## Health and safety (Sections 19–22)
- Drinking water, conservancy (sanitation/latrines), medical appliances and
  first-aid must be provided (Sections 19–21).
- Powers exist to make rules on health and safety, ventilation, dust, lighting,
  support of workings, machinery and so on.

## Hours and limitation of employment
- Section 28: A weekly day of rest must be provided; a worker is not to work for
  more than six days in any week.
- Section 30 (above ground): No adult worker employed above ground may be
  required or allowed to work more than 48 hours in any week, or more than 9
  hours in any day.
- Section 31 (below ground): No adult worker may be employed below ground for
  more than 48 hours in any week, or more than 8 hours in any day; below-ground
  work is organised through a system of shifts.
- Section 33: A worker who works beyond the daily or weekly limits is entitled to
  overtime wages at twice the ordinary rate of wages.
- Section 34: Limits on spread-over and night shifts.

## Employment of young persons and women
- Section 40: No person below 18 years of age shall be allowed to work in any
  mine or part of a mine. (Apprentices and trainees above 16 may be allowed under
  specified conditions and supervision.)
- Section 45: Prohibition of the presence of persons below 18 years in any part
  of a mine where mining operations are carried on.
- Section 46: Women are prohibited from working below ground in any mine; and
  above ground they may generally be employed only between 6 a.m. and 7 p.m.
  (the government may relax the timing in certain cases).

## Leave with wages (Sections 52–56)
- Adult workers earn annual leave with wages of one day for every 20 days of work
  performed (one day for every 15 days for persons below 18 years), subject to
  the conditions in the Act.

## Penalties
- The Act prescribes penalties (fines and/or imprisonment) for contravention of
  its provisions, obstruction of inspectors, failure to give notice of accidents,
  and for owners, agents and managers who fail in their duties.
