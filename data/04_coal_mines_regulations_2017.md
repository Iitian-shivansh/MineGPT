# The Coal Mines Regulations, 2017 — Key Provisions

The Coal Mines Regulations, 2017 (CMR 2017) were made under Section 57 of the
Mines Act, 1952. They came into force on 27 November 2017 and superseded the
earlier Coal Mines Regulations, 1957. CMR 2017 is the main body of technical
safety regulation for coal mines in India.

## Modern safety-management approach
A central feature of CMR 2017 is a shift towards a risk-based, management-system
approach, requiring:
- A **Safety Management Plan (SMP)** based on formal **risk assessment** of the
  mine's hazards, with controls for the principal hazards.
- Clearly documented systems, responsibilities and competencies.
- Principal hazard management plans (for example for strata control, inundation,
  fire and explosion, gas, dust, mine ventilation, and mechanical/electrical
  engineering).

## Notice of accidents and dangerous occurrences (Regulation 8)
- Regulation 8 requires notice of accidents and dangerous occurrences. When an
  accident causing loss of life or serious bodily injury occurs in or about a
  mine in connection with any mining operation, notice must be given to the
  prescribed authorities (this gives effect to Section 23 of the Mines Act).
- Regulation 251 requires that the site of an accident is not to be disturbed,
  except to save life, prevent further injury or for essential reasons, until
  cleared by the competent authority.

## Key personnel and duties
- The regulations define the duties of the Owner, Agent and Manager, and of
  statutory officials such as the Assistant Manager, Engineer, Overman, Sirdar
  (mining mate), Shotfirer and Surveyor.
- Only persons holding the appropriate competency certificate granted by DGMS may
  hold statutory positions (for example Manager, Overman, Sirdar, Surveyor).

## Ventilation
- Adequate ventilation must be provided to dilute and render harmless inflammable
  and noxious gases and dust, and to provide fresh air to all working places.
- Standards are set for minimum air quantity and quality, and for limiting
  concentrations of gases such as methane (firedamp), carbon dioxide (blackdamp)
  and carbon monoxide.

## Gas and explosion safety
- Systematic testing for inflammable gas using approved flame safety lamps or
  methanometers at prescribed intervals.
- Restrictions on the use of lamps, lights and electrical apparatus in gassy
  seams; mines are classified by degree of gassiness.
- Controls on blasting/shotfiring, use of permitted explosives, and stone-dusting
  to prevent coal-dust explosions.

## Support of workings and strata control
- Systematic support rules must be framed and displayed; roof and sides of
  workings must be supported to prevent falls of roof and sides (a major cause of
  accidents).

## Inundation, fire and emergency
- Precautions against inundation (water danger) including maintaining barriers and
  dealing with old / waterlogged workings.
- Precautions against fire, spontaneous heating and explosions.
- Emergency Response and Disaster Management Plan, means of escape, and provision
  for rescue and recovery.

## Records, plans and surveys
- Accurate mine plans and sections must be prepared and kept up to date by a
  qualified surveyor, and surrendered/updated as required.
