# The Mines Rules, 1955 — Key Provisions

The Mines Rules, 1955 are framed under the Mines Act, 1952 and deal mainly with
the welfare, health and medical facilities of mine workers, and with the
administration of the Act.

## Welfare amenities
- Drinking water: Provision of a sufficient supply of cool, wholesome drinking
  water at convenient points.
- Conservancy: Provision and maintenance of latrines and urinals, separately for
  men and women.
- First-aid: First-aid boxes and appliances must be provided and kept readily
  accessible; a prescribed number of trained first-aid persons must be available
  on each shift.
- Rest shelters and canteens are required in mines employing workers above
  specified thresholds.

## Medical examination
- The Rules require initial (pre-employment) and periodical medical examination
  of persons employed in mines, to assess fitness for mining work.
- Records of medical examinations must be maintained, and persons found unfit may
  be restricted from certain work.

## Occupational health
- Provisions relate to the prevention and surveillance of occupational diseases
  such as pneumoconiosis (including silicosis and coal workers' pneumoconiosis),
  noise-induced hearing loss and others notifiable under the Act.

## Safety Committees and Workmen's Inspectors
- Mines are required to constitute a Safety Committee with representatives of
  management and workers to promote safety and review safety performance.
- Workmen's Inspectors (safety, health) are appointed from among workers to
  inspect the mine and report unsafe conditions.

## Notice boards and records
- A special notice board and various statutory registers and records must be
  maintained and kept available for inspection by DGMS officers.
