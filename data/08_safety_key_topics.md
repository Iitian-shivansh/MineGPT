# Key Safety Topics and Hazards in Indian Mines (DGMS Focus Areas)

## Principal causes of accidents
DGMS accident analysis consistently identifies the leading causes of fatal mining
accidents, which include:
- **Fall of roof and sides (ground movement)** — a major cause in underground
  coal and metal mines. Controlled by systematic support rules.
- **Haulage and transport** — rope haulage, conveyors, dumpers and trucks.
- **Explosives / blasting** — misfires, premature blasts, flyrock.
- **Dust and gas explosions** (coal) — methane ignition and coal-dust propagation.
- **Inundation** — sudden inrush of water from old workings or surface water.
- **Machinery** — moving parts, HEMM operation in opencast mines.
- **Electricity** — shock, arcing and fire.

## Ground / strata control
- Systematic Support Rules (SSR) must be framed, approved and displayed.
- Roof bolting, props, chocks, cogs and steel supports are used per the SSR.
- Scaling / dressing of loose rock before work, and regular inspection of roof
  and sides.

## Ventilation and gases (coal)
- Firedamp (methane, CH4): inflammable; mines are classified by gassiness
  (degree I, II, III). Continuous monitoring and limits apply.
- Blackdamp (stythe): oxygen-deficient air with excess CO2 / nitrogen.
- Afterdamp: gases, chiefly carbon monoxide, left after an explosion or fire.
- Whitedamp (carbon monoxide, CO): highly toxic even in small concentrations.
- Stinkdamp (hydrogen sulphide, H2S): toxic, rotten-egg smell.
- Flame safety lamps and methanometers are used to detect inflammable gas.

## Dust control and occupational health
- Airborne respirable dust causes pneumoconiosis: coal workers' pneumoconiosis
  (CWP) in coal, and silicosis in silica-bearing metal mines and quarries.
- Controls: wet drilling, water spraying, dust extraction, stone-dusting (coal),
  and respiratory protection.
- Noise, vibration and heat are also monitored occupational hazards.
- Periodical medical examination (PME) and initial medical examination (IME) are
  required to detect occupational disease early.

## Explosives and blasting
- Only permitted explosives and approved detonators and accessories may be used,
  especially in gassy coal seams.
- Shots are fired only by authorised shotfirers/blasters following prescribed
  charging, stemming, firing and misfire procedures.
- Explosives must be stored in licensed magazines and accounted for.

## Emergency preparedness
- Emergency Response Plan / Disaster Management Plan.
- Means of escape (at least two separate ways out of underground workings),
  self-rescuers for workers, and refuge chambers where applicable.
- Mines Rescue arrangements (see Mines Rescue Rules, 1985).
- Fire-fighting arrangements and firefighting training.

## Safety management
- Risk assessment and a Safety Management Plan (emphasised in CMR 2017).
- Safety Committee and Workmen's Inspectors.
- Safety audits, internal safety organisation, and regular statutory inspections
  by officials and by DGMS.
- Reporting of accidents and dangerous occurrences, and acting on inquiry
  findings and DGMS circulars/recommendations.
