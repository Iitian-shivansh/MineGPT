# Mines Rescue Rules, 1985 and Mines Vocational Training Rules, 1966

## The Mines Rescue Rules, 1985
These Rules provide for organised rescue arrangements in mines, especially those
liable to the danger of fire, explosion or irrespirable atmosphere.

Key points:
- **Rescue Stations and Rescue Rooms:** Mines (particularly coal and gassy mines)
  must be served by a Mines Rescue Station, and larger mines must maintain a
  Rescue Room with apparatus.
- **Rescue brigades:** A sufficient number of trained rescue-brigade members
  (rescue-trained persons) must be available. Members use breathing apparatus and
  are trained to work in irrespirable atmospheres.
- **Training and re-training:** Rescue personnel must undergo initial training and
  periodical refresher/re-training and practice to remain qualified.
- **Medical fitness:** Rescue workers must be medically examined and certified fit
  for the physical demands of rescue work.
- **Apparatus:** Self-contained breathing apparatus, reviving apparatus, gas
  detectors and other rescue equipment must be maintained in working order and
  regularly tested.
- **Emergency response:** Arrangements for prompt mobilisation of rescue teams in
  the event of an emergency.

## The Mines Vocational Training Rules, 1966
These Rules make training compulsory before a person is employed in a mine and at
various stages of employment.

Key points:
- **Basic training:** A new (previously untrained) worker must undergo initial
  basic vocational training at a recognised Vocational Training Centre (VTC)
  before being deployed on a job.
- **Job / skill training:** Practical training for the specific job the worker
  will perform.
- **Refresher training:** Periodical refresher training for existing workers to
  update their skills and safety knowledge.
- **Special training:** When a worker is transferred to a new type of work, or
  new machinery/technology is introduced, special training must be given.
- **Records:** Training records and certificates must be maintained and produced
  for inspection.
- The purpose is to ensure that no worker is deployed without being trained in the
  hazards of the mine and the safe method of work.
